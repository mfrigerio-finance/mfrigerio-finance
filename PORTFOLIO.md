# Professional Portfolio

**Finance · Transformation · Enterprise Systems · Data · Architecture**

This is a public, sanitised view of professional responsibilities, methods, systems exposure and documented outcomes across finance, transformation and technology-enabled delivery.

Most client work is subject to confidentiality obligations. Client identities, production data, credentials, private infrastructure and proprietary source code are therefore excluded.

## 1. Executive Finance & Performance

Professional scope includes:

- strategic finance and CFO-office support;
- finance business partnering and commercial decision support;
- budgeting, rolling forecasts and driver-based planning;
- scenario and sensitivity analysis;
- variance, margin, profitability and cost-driver analysis;
- KPI frameworks and executive reporting;
- CAPEX / OPEX analysis;
- investment appraisal and capital-allocation support.

The operating objective is to connect forward-looking decisions with controlled financial evidence rather than producing reporting in isolation.

## 2. Accounting & Group Finance

Professional materials include work across:

- GL, AP/AR, accruals and fixed assets;
- month-end, quarter-end and year-end close;
- reconciliations and reporting packages;
- intercompany, consolidation and eliminations;
- currency translation;
- statutory and management reporting;
- VAT and audit support;
- internal controls, audit trails and evidence packs.

Accounting and reporting exposure spans Swiss and international environments, including **IFRS, Swiss GAAP FER and statutory reporting contexts**. Framework and jurisdiction ownership varies by mandate.

## 3. Treasury, Capital & Corporate Finance

Professional scope includes:

- liquidity planning and cash positioning;
- working-capital analysis and optimisation;
- banking relationships and international settlements;
- credit, facilities and covenant considerations;
- funding strategy and capital-structure analysis;
- FX, interest-rate, liquidity and counterparty-risk considerations;
- valuation, DCF, NPV, IRR and WACC;
- investment appraisal, due diligence and scenario analysis.

The focus is on making assumptions, sensitivities, cash implications and risk visible to decision-makers.

## 4. Finance Transformation & Enterprise Systems

Finance transformation is treated as a chain connecting **policy, process, master data, systems, integrations, controls, reporting and user behaviour**.

Typical delivery scope includes:

- finance operating-model and target-state design;
- as-is / to-be process mapping;
- process redesign and standardisation;
- requirements gathering and functional specifications;
- acceptance criteria and UAT;
- defect analysis and remediation;
- integration and data-flow design;
- controls, segregation of duties and auditability;
- documentation, training and adoption;
- continuous improvement based on exceptions and root causes.

## 5. Data, BI & Automation

Analytics is treated as part of the finance control environment, not as a dashboard layer detached from source data.

Professional scope includes:

- SQL and relational data modelling;
- ETL / ELT concepts and workflows;
- master data, ownership and governance;
- data lineage and provenance;
- reconciliation and data-quality controls;
- Power BI and KPI design;
- Python and quantitative analysis;
- Advanced Excel, Power Query and VBA;
- workflow automation and exception handling.

A documented professional engagement reported a **40% reduction in manual reporting time** through Excel, Power Query and VBA automation.

## 6. Systems Architecture & Technical Delivery

Hands-on technical work supports finance and transformation requirements across:

- Python;
- SQL;
- TypeScript / JavaScript;
- FastAPI and REST APIs;
- PostgreSQL and relational data models;
- typed interfaces and validation;
- modular service and domain boundaries;
- workflow and state logic;
- authorization, isolation and auditability;
- automated verification and technical documentation.

The purpose of technical fluency is practical: make requirements more precise, integrations more testable, automation safer and communication between finance and engineering more effective.

## 7. Control-by-Design

Across finance, data and systems work, recurring control themes include:

- source and data ownership;
- approvals and segregation of duties;
- reconciliations;
- audit trails and traceability;
- validation and acceptance criteria;
- change control;
- exception visibility;
- fail-closed behaviour where an unknown or inconsistent state could produce a misleading output.

## International operating perspective

Professional materials represent cross-border finance and reporting contexts spanning Switzerland, Europe, North America, the Gulf and selected Asia-Pacific environments.

This should be read as international professional coverage rather than a claim of sole statutory ownership in every jurisdiction.

## Core proposition

The common thread is the ability to connect:

**Financial meaning → Operating reality → Controls → Data → Systems → Management decision**

That combination is particularly useful in finance transformation, performance management, group finance, finance systems and technically complex CFO-office environments.

## Selected evidence

- International professional mandates since 2019 across finance, transformation, technology and cross-border execution.
- CFO-level decision support, planning, forecasting, liquidity, investment evaluation and executive reporting.
- Finance and reporting workflows connecting accounting, operations, ERP systems and financial/non-financial data.
- **40% stated reduction in manual reporting time** through Excel, Power Query and VBA automation.
- Cross-functional delivery spanning finance requirements, process design, data, controls, systems and implementation detail.
