# Sanitised Professional Case Studies

These case studies describe professional delivery without client names, confidential operating data, credentials, private infrastructure or proprietary source code.

They are intentionally written around **problem → delivery → control focus → value** rather than around client-specific implementation detail.

## 1. Finance & Reporting Automation

### Problem
Recurring management reporting required substantial manual preparation across spreadsheets, source data and reporting outputs. The process consumed time and increased the risk of inconsistent handling between reporting cycles.

### Delivery
- Structured the workflow around explicit source inputs, transformations and management outputs.
- Used Excel, Power Query and VBA to reduce repetitive manual handling.
- Preserved links between source data, reporting logic and financial meaning.
- Kept exception handling and reconciliation visible rather than hiding differences inside the output.

### Control focus
- source ownership;
- reconciliation;
- repeatability;
- exception visibility;
- traceability from input to management output.

### Documented value
A professional engagement reported a **40% reduction in manual reporting time**.

### Capabilities demonstrated
FP&A · management reporting · automation · reconciliation · process improvement · Excel · Power Query · VBA

## 2. Finance & ERP Transformation

### Problem
Finance requirements, operational processes, master data, integrations and system behaviour needed to remain aligned during transformation. A platform change alone would not solve inconsistencies in ownership, controls or reporting logic.

### Delivery
- Mapped as-is processes, pain points, stakeholders and ownership.
- Translated finance requirements into target processes, functional requirements and acceptance criteria.
- Connected accounting logic, master data, integrations and reporting requirements.
- Supported UAT, defect analysis, remediation, reconciliation and control validation.
- Documented decisions and operating procedures to support adoption.

### Control focus
- segregation of duties;
- approvals;
- data ownership;
- reconciliation;
- acceptance criteria;
- auditability;
- change and defect control.

### Value delivered
A more controlled path from finance requirement to system behaviour, reducing ambiguity between business users, technical teams and the final reporting output.

### Capabilities demonstrated
Finance transformation · SAP/ERP environments · requirements · process design · integration · UAT · controls · data · adoption

## 3. Executive Finance & Decision Support

### Problem
Management decisions required financial views that connected operating assumptions, P&L impact, cash implications, scenarios and investment economics rather than isolated accounting outputs.

### Delivery
- Built budgets, forecasts and scenarios around explicit drivers and assumptions.
- Connected P&L, cash-flow, liquidity and working-capital implications.
- Applied valuation and investment metrics including DCF, NPV, IRR and WACC where relevant.
- Structured KPI and management-reporting views for executive decision support.
- Kept assumptions, sensitivities and exceptions visible for review.

### Control focus
- assumption ownership;
- scenario consistency;
- sensitivity visibility;
- reconciliation to financial outputs;
- clear separation between fact, assumption and forecast.

### Value delivered
Decision material designed to explain not only **what** the financial result was, but **why**, what could change it and which management action was available.

### Capabilities demonstrated
Strategic finance · FP&A · treasury · corporate finance · valuation · scenario modelling · executive reporting

## 4. Finance Data, Controls & Reconciliation

### Problem
Financial and non-financial information from multiple sources needed to remain consistent, explainable and usable for reporting and control purposes. A technically successful data transfer would not be sufficient if accounting meaning or source ownership was lost.

### Delivery
- Defined source ownership and mapping logic.
- Structured validation and reconciliation checks before reporting.
- Connected operational and financial data while preserving accounting meaning.
- Made exceptions and unresolved items visible instead of silently defaulting them.
- Supported reporting workflows spanning finance, data and systems stakeholders.

### Control focus
- provenance;
- mapping ownership;
- data-quality validation;
- reconciliation;
- exception management;
- traceability into management reporting.

### Value delivered
A clearer and more controlled path from source data to decision-ready reporting, with unresolved states kept visible to the people responsible for resolving them.

### Capabilities demonstrated
Finance data architecture · reconciliation · controls · reporting · data quality · cross-functional delivery

## Working pattern across professional mandates

The recurring delivery model is:

**Frame → Analyse → Design → Deliver → Validate → Improve**

Across finance, transformation and systems work, the same principles recur:

- financial meaning should remain connected to source evidence;
- assumptions and ownership should be explicit;
- controls should sit where they can actually be enforced;
- unresolved or inconsistent states should remain visible;
- management outputs should be explainable and reconcilable;
- technology should support the operating model rather than obscure it.

## Confidentiality boundary

These case studies intentionally omit client names, exact financial values, private system architecture, credentials, production endpoints and proprietary implementation detail.

The purpose is to show **professional reasoning, delivery scope and control discipline** without presenting confidential work as public portfolio code.
